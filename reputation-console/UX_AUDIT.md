# Reputation Console — UX/UI Audit & Redesign Plan

_Audience: a non-technical business/finance owner. Produced from a 10-agent review of the live components against the owner's verbatim feedback (2026-06-16)._

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Reputation Console is structurally sound at the data layer but fails its target user — a non-technical finance owner — at the presentation layer. Across all nine areas the same failures recur. (1) RICH DATA IS THROWN AWAY: the dashboard fetches data.gap/plan/attribution/before_after and renders none of it; SEO computes per-page semantic recs and sub-scores and shows none; Gaps, Time-to-Goal, Rankings, and Integrations dump structured, decision-ready payloads through DataBlocks — a generic JSON-to-bullets dumper — producing equally-weighted walls of text on the four most strategic pages. (2) TWO CONTRADICTORY SCALES: the engine's raw goal_alignment (-1..+1) leaks onto the Audits index, the trend chart, Time-to-Goal, and Rankings as decimals like 0.34, directly contradicting the 0-100 reputation score shown everywhere else. (3) NO PLAIN-ENGLISH VERDICT: numbers appear with no 'what does this mean / is it good or bad' sentence, no 'why it matters', and unexplained jargon everywhere (grounded, contested, schema, share of voice, semantic readiness, SLA, relevance 0.87, openai_search, wo_code, gain per unit shipped). (4) THE PROBLEM→ACTION→TRACKING→RESULT THREAD IS BROKEN AT THE UI even though the data already supports it end-to-end: the gap model that drives Gaps is the same model strategy_generator turns into Work Orders and production_brief turns into briefs/drafts, but no page links a problem to its task or a task back to its gap. (5) NO 'WHEN DO I RECHECK': projected dates and next-audit cadence exist server-side but never surface, so the score feels static. (6) NO ACTION-CENTER: the owner must visit five pages and assemble their own to-do list. (7) DEAD-END EMPTY STATES: blank gray sentences read as 'the product is broken.' THE THROUGH-LINE FIX: convert every raw number to the 0-100 score, lead every surface with a plain-English verdict + 'why it matters', replace DataBlocks with a headline-first progressive-disclosure pattern, hard-wire every metric to a 'What to do →' link and every gap/problem to its existing work order, surface projected/next-recheck dates, and rebuild the dashboard as the hub that answers the owner's five questions and threads the whole product into one story.

## CROSS-CUTTING PRINCIPLES
- One scale, always 0-100: never show the raw -1..+1 goal_alignment to this user. Every score on every page runs through repScore() and carries its band word (Poor/Weak/Neutral/Strong/Excellent) and the 50=neutral anchor. Kill the contradictory decimals on Audits, the trend chart, Time-to-Goal, and Rankings.
- Verdict before data: every metric-heavy surface opens with one plain-English, color-coded sentence answering 'what does this mean and is it good or bad' BEFORE any chart or table (the highest-leverage fix for 'hard to understand').
- Why-it-matters on every metric: no number appears without a one-line plain-English consequence ('AI fills the gap from forums you don't control'). This is a first-class field, not a tiny gray footnote.
- Progressive disclosure — highlights first, details on click: replace DataBlocks everywhere with a Headline + 1-3 highlight stats visible, raw detail behind 'See details'. Default-open only the top 1-2 highest-severity sections.
- Plain language over jargon, with an inline glossary: real product names (ChatGPT not openai_search), worded labels (Match: High not 0.73), and a reusable <Term> tooltip backed by one shared glossary so jargon is never unexplained.
- Good/bad color is consistent and explained: green = good for your reputation, red = working against you, gray = neutral — with a one-line legend on metric pages. The component (not each page) owns inverted metrics like contested-down-is-good via goodDirection.
- Every problem links to its action, every action to its tracking: 'What to do →' is a required field on the canonical metric card and on every section headline; every gap/weak-answer/contested-source/lever resolves to its existing work order with a live status badge.
- Always answer 'when do I recheck': a standardized freshness/recheck chip ('Last audited Jun 14 · next recheck ~Jul 14') on every data surface, plus projected target dates wherever the engine computes them.
- Empty and learning states guide, never dead-end: explain why it's empty, what produces the data, expected timing, and a primary CTA (or a spinner+ETA when a job is running).

## INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
Relabel the six nav groups to the owner's own questions and reorder so action sits second, right after diagnosis. Recommended nav (hrefs unchanged to avoid routing churn): (1) HOME — the dashboard/action hub. (2) WHAT AI SAYS ABOUT YOU — Audits (latest run summary + worst answers), Your Gaps, Your Website (SEO). (3) YOUR ACTION PLAN [pull up from third] — Tasks (work orders), Content (briefs+drafts), Outreach; show a 'Needs you: N' badge. (4) PROGRESS — Rankings/Share of voice, Time to Goal. (5) PROTECT & MONITOR — Incidents, Mentions, What's Working (levers). (6) CONNECTED DATA — Integrations. Add a new HOME-level 'Do this next' surface (the action-center) that aggregates open work orders + gap-recommended assets + the top acceleration lever into one ranked checklist.\n\nTHE NARRATIVE THREAD (one story, each page hands off to the next): SCORE (Home: 'AI leans Weak about you, 34/100, mostly because they don't know you yet') → WHAT AI SAYS (Audits: the worst answers and per-engine read that produced that score; SEO: why your site is hard to cite) → GAPS (the named, plain-English reasons, each with severity) → TASKS (each gap's existing work order → brief → draft → published, as a visible pipeline) → TIMELINE (when those tasks move the score, with a projected finish date) → PROGRESS (Rankings momentum + trend confirm it moved) → SUSTAIN (Incidents/Mentions/Levers keep it). Every page carries a back-link to the problem it serves and a forward-link to the next step, and every problem row resolves to a status-bearing task. The join is already in the data model (gap model → strategy_generator work orders → production_brief drafts; work_order_id on drafts); the redesign just surfaces it.

## DESIGN SYSTEM
### Canonical MetricCard (replaces the one-off KpiStatCard; lives in ui.tsx)
Props: {label (plain English), value (string), tone:'good'|'bad'|'neutral', delta?, goodDirection?:'up'|'down', timeframe?, whyItMatters (required one-liner), action?:{label,href}, info?:term}. ALWAYS answers the five questions: plain label; big 0-100 value; a tone chip (green/red/gray from repScore TONE_CLASSES) that reads good/bad at a glance; a worded, timeframed delta ('+6 pts since last audit', direction-aware via goodDirection so contested-down shows green); a 'Why:' line in normal-weight text (not tiny gray); and a 'What to do →' link. Migrate all KpiStatCard callers, then delete KpiStatCard. The decimal delta (toFixed(2)) and the 12px gray hint are removed.
### DataSection — the progressive-disclosure replacement for DataBlocks (in ui.tsx)
Props: {title, headline (plain-English verdict), severity:'high'|'med'|'low'|'good', highlights:[{label,value,tone}], defaultOpen, children}. Renders: a scored/colored section header, the one-line headline, 1-3 highlight MetricCards, then the raw/long content behind a collapsed 'See the full breakdown' <details>. DataBlocks is retained ONLY inside that collapsed <details> as a raw fallback for unknown shapes (and behind a dev flag). Each strategic page (Gaps, Time-to-Goal, Rankings, Integrations, SEO) gets a thin typed adapter that maps its known payload keys into headline+highlights instead of dumping every key. This is the single highest-leverage component — it kills the wall of text on the four worst pages.
### Color/tone semantics + visible legend
One exported toneClasses(tone) + <ToneLegend/> derived from repScore TONE_CLASSES. Rule app-wide: green=good for your reputation, red=working against you, gray=neutral. A one-line legend renders under PageHeader on metric pages. Inverted metrics (contested, recognition gap, weeks-saved) compute tone from goodDirection inside the component, never per-page, so red always means 'bad for you.' The 0-100 band gradient gets a plain '50 = neutral' anchor label.
### Glossary <Term> tooltip + /glossary page
A <Term name> wrapper renders a dotted-underline term with a click/hover popover (works on touch, unlike title=) showing {plain_english, why_it_matters} from one shared GLOSSARY map. Replaces all ad-hoc HTML title= usages. A /glossary reference page lists every term. Seed from the consolidated glossary (score, contested, owned, grounded, schema, share of voice, semantic readiness, recognition gap, work order, beat, relevance, SLA, normalize, etc.).
### Action-center / 'Do this next'
A NextSteps component (and a /next-steps page) that aggregates open work orders + gap-model recommended assets + the top acceleration lever, ranked by impact, each row showing plain title, an 'expected lift' chip, owner (You / Koob team), live status badge, and a deep link to its owning page. The top 3 surface on Home above the fold. This is the single place the owner's plan lives, ending the 'visit five pages and assemble it yourself' problem.
### Freshness/recheck chip
<Freshness asOf cadenceDays/> renders 'Last audited Jun 14 · next recheck ~Jul 14' (and a projected target date where the engine provides one). Placed in MetricCard/DataSection headers and page headers. Reuses existing created_at/finished_at; answers the owner's fifth question consistently.
### Per-engine humanization (shared ENGINE_LABELS)
One ENGINE_LABELS map (openai_search→ChatGPT, anthropic→Claude, perplexity→Perplexity, gemini→Gemini) used by PerEnginePanel, the audit per-engine strip, and gap/weak-query engine chips. Each engine tile shows a 0-100 chip + one-word state (Favorable / Neutral / Doesn't know you / Wrong business). Statistics (95% CI) move behind an info tooltip; 'grounded' → 'Answered from a live web search.'
### Instructive empty/learning states
A standard EmptyState({why, produces, timing, cta}) replacing every bare gray sentence. Three flavors: first-run ('Run my first audit' CTA + bullet preview of what will appear), job-in-progress (spinner + ETA), and learning ('We need a second audit to rank this — next ~{date}'). Used on dashboard, work orders, drafts, briefs, levers, integrations, momentum, and the trend chart.
### Typography & density
Promote 'why it matters' and verdict copy out of 12px gray micro-text into normal-weight readable body. PageHeader gains an optional whatItIsFor line (normal weight) + ToneLegend slot, so framing like 'this is a projection, not a promise' is no longer skippable gray. Default-collapse completed/low-priority content (Done/Verified columns, detail metrics) to reduce the equally-weighted-cards overwhelm.

## GLOSSARY
- **Reputation score (0-100)** — How favorably AI assistants talk about you, on a 0-100 scale. 50 means neutral or they don't know you; below 50 leans unfavorable, above 50 leans favorable.  (_why it matters:_ It's the single headline number; the owner must instantly grasp the scale and what 'good' looks like. Never show the raw -1..+1 goal_alignment.)
- **goal_alignment / current_alignment** — The engine's internal -1..+1 version of the reputation score — always convert it to the 0-100 score before showing it.  (_why it matters:_ Shown raw (e.g. 0.34) it contradicts the 0-100 score everywhere else and is meaningless to a non-technical owner.)
- **Owned content / owned sources** — How often AI answers cite YOUR own website or pages (vs other sites).  (_why it matters:_ Higher is good — it means AI is repeating your accurate version of the story, which you control.)
- **Contested** — AI answers that bring up a dispute, complaint, scam/ripoff claim, or negative topic about you.  (_why it matters:_ These work against your reputation; lower is good. The word is jargon — frame as 'raising concerns / working against you.')
- **Grounded** — The AI answered using a live web search instead of its memory.  (_why it matters:_ Grounded answers are more current and can be changed by publishing new content; memory-based ones are stickier — this shapes the fix.)
- **95% confidence interval** — The likely range for a number given how many answers we sampled.  (_why it matters:_ Stops over-reacting to a score based on only a few answers; hide behind an info tooltip, never show inline.)
- **Recognition / awareness gap** — AI doesn't know your business well yet — an information void to fill.  (_why it matters:_ This is the FASTER problem to fix (publish accurate content); it determines which actions to take.)
- **Negative narrative** — AI knows you but says unfavorable things.  (_why it matters:_ The SLOWER problem — needs accurate content to outweigh the negative, plus rebuttals; drives different actions than an awareness gap.)
- **Entity confusion / wrong entity** — AI is mixing you up with a different business that shares your name.  (_why it matters:_ A concrete, high-priority, fixable error that can attach someone else's reputation to you.)
- **Schema / structured data / JSON-LD** — Hidden labels in your website's code that spell out your official name, reviews, and FAQs so AI reads them correctly.  (_why it matters:_ Without them AI guesses your details and may use wrong or third-party info; '0 of 5' means none exist.)
- **Semantic / citation readiness** — How easy it is for AI to read your pages and quote a clear answer about you (0-100).  (_why it matters:_ If AI can't pull a clean answer from your site, it answers from forums and reviews you don't control. Healthy sites score 60+.)
- **Front-loading** — Putting the key answer near the top of a page instead of burying it.  (_why it matters:_ AI tends to quote the top of a page, so the answer needs to be there to get cited.)
- **Freshness** — Whether a page shows a recent date.  (_why it matters:_ Recent-looking content gets cited roughly twice as often; stale pages get skipped.)
- **Missing topics (entities)** — Subjects AI expects a business like yours to cover, that your site barely mentions.  (_why it matters:_ When you don't cover a topic AI expects, AI fills the gap from outside sources and you lose control of that part of your story.)
- **Thin corroboration** — Claims you make that no outside source backs up yet — so AI stays vague about them.  (_why it matters:_ AI trusts third-party proof, not self-claims; you need outside sources (license registry, press) to confirm them.)
- **Share of voice** — Of every website AI quotes about you, what slice is yours (Owned) vs neutral vs critical (Contested).  (_why it matters:_ Tells you whether AI is mostly repeating accurate sources or critical ones — the heart of your AI reputation.)
- **Class / classification (Owned / Neutral / Contested)** — The TYPE of source AI cited: your own site, an independent third party, or a complaint/scam site.  (_why it matters:_ You want AI quoting Owned and Neutral; Contested sources spread the bad narrative.)
- **Cites / Share** — Cites = how many times AI quoted that website about you; Share = that site's slice of all citations.  (_why it matters:_ More cites/share = more influence that source has over what AI says about you.)
- **Momentum (rising/falling/new/dropped)** — Whether a source is being cited more, less, for the first time, or no longer — vs your last audit.  (_why it matters:_ Shows if good sources are winning and bad ones fading — i.e. whether your work is paying off.)
- **Work order** — A task — a specific job to improve your reputation that you or the Koob team will do.  (_why it matters:_ This is the ACTION unit that ties a problem to a concrete, trackable next step.)
- **wo_code / phase** — A reference number for a task, and which stage of the plan it's in ('Stage 2 of the plan').  (_why it matters:_ Codes look like errors; demote to a small caption and label phase plainly.)
- **Capability** — The type of work a task is (e.g. 'Website content', 'PR outreach').  (_why it matters:_ Internal taxonomy the owner can't parse; needs a plain category label.)
- **Status: Done vs Verified** — Done = content created; Verified = we re-checked and AI engines actually improved.  (_why it matters:_ The owner needs to know which status means the result actually landed.)
- **Quality score** — How well-written and on-brand a draft is, shown as Strong/OK/Weak (e.g. 82/100).  (_why it matters:_ A bare 0-1 float gives no sense of good/bad; a word + color does.)
- **Production brief / target_query** — A content recipe — what to make and what question it answers when someone asks AI.  (_why it matters:_ Connects each piece of content directly to the reputation problem it fixes.)
- **Outreach score / beat** — Match: High/Medium (how relevant an outlet is to you); beat = the topic a journalist covers.  (_why it matters:_ A raw 0.73 and 'beat' are meaningless; outreach earns mentions AI engines read.)
- **Dominance target** — The finish line — the point where AI mostly says accurate, positive things about you (shown as a 0-100 goal).  (_why it matters:_ An internal threshold the owner has never seen; needs a plain 'finish line' label.)
- **Confidence (low/medium/high)** — How sure we are of the projected date — it sharpens after each audit.  (_why it matters:_ Unexplained 'low' reads as incompetence rather than 'we'll firm this up after your next audit.')
- **Acceleration levers / weeks saved** — Outside actions (guest articles, reviews, press) that finish you sooner, and how many weeks each cuts off.  (_why it matters:_ The actual to-do list to speed things up; 'levers' is jargon and the weeks-saved payoff must be visible.)
- **Gain per unit shipped / baseline monthly gain** — How much your score rises each time you do one more of an action / how much it drifts on its own per month.  (_why it matters:_ As a raw coefficient it's unreadable; expressed as '+points' it tells the owner where to spend effort.)
- **SLA** — The recommended deadline to respond — e.g. 'respond by Thursday 6pm.'  (_why it matters:_ 'SLA 24h' is corporate jargon; an absolute deadline tells them when they actually need to act.)
- **Relevance** — How sure we are a mention is about your business — 'Strong match' / 'Likely about you' / 'Possibly unrelated.'  (_why it matters:_ A raw 0.87 float means nothing; a label lets the owner dismiss noise.)
- **Normalize / signal_type (technical_seo, serp_rank, backlinks)** — Turn a pasted report into data we can use; report types are 'Technical SEO health', 'Google ranking', 'Links to your site.'  (_why it matters:_ Data-engineering terms and raw enums force the owner to guess; plain labels make uploading worthwhile.)
- **Status: raw / normalized / failed** — 'Ready to process' / 'Processed' / 'Couldn't read this.'  (_why it matters:_ Status should describe what the owner can do, not the internal pipeline state.)
- **Partial coverage** — Only some of the 4 AI engines were checked this round, so the picture is partial.  (_why it matters:_ Changes how much to trust the numbers; must be explained, not shown as a bare fraction.)
- **Engine keys (openai_search / anthropic)** — ChatGPT / Claude — use the product names, not internal keys.  (_why it matters:_ Raw keys look like a bug and mean nothing to the owner.)
